Remember that analysis is for a large part about making rigorous statements about the approximate behavior of functions? The following definition is a rigorous statement about the behavior of a sequence $a: \mathbb{N} \to X$, where $(X, \dist)$ a metric space. The definition is a precise version of the following approximate statement: for large $n$, the distance between $a_n$ and $p$ is small.

\begin{definition}
Let $(X, \dist)$ be a metric space. We say that a sequence $a : \mathbb{N} \to X$ converges to a point $p \in X$ if
\[
\begin{split}
&\foralltext \epsilon  > 0,\\
&\quad  \existstext N \in \mathbb{N}, \\
&\quad \quad \foralltext n \geq N, \\
&\quad \quad \quad \dist(a_n, p) < \epsilon.
\end{split}
\]
\end{definition}

We sometimes write
\[
\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n = p
\]
to express that the sequence $(a_n)$ converges to $p$.

\begin{example}
Let's see what this definition looks like when the metric space $(X, \dist)$ is $(\mathbb{R}, \dist_{\mathbb{R}})$, where by $\dist_{\mathbb{R}}: \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ we always mean the standard distance on $\mathbb{R}$ given by
\[
\dist_{\mathbb{R}}(x, y) = | x - y |.
\]
A sequence $a : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{R}$ then converges to $L \in \mathbb{R}$ if and only if
\[
\begin{split}
& \foralltext \epsilon > 0,\\
& \quad \existstext N \in \mathbb{N},\\
& \quad\quad \foralltext n \geq N,\\
&\quad\quad\quad |a_n - L| < \epsilon.
\end{split}
\]
\end{example}

\begin{definition}
Let $(X, \dist)$ be a metric space. A sequence $a : \mathbb{N} \to X$ is called \emph{divergent} if it is not convergent.	
\end{definition}
