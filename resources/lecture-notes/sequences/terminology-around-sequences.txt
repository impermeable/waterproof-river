\begin{definition}[bounded sequences]
	\label{de:bounded-sequence}
Let $(X, \dist)$ be a metric space.
We say a sequence $a: \mathbb{N} \to X$ is \emph{bounded} if
\[
\begin{split}
& \existstext q \in X,\\
& \quad	\existstext M > 0, \\
& \quad\quad \foralltext n \in \mathbb{N}, \\
& \quad\quad\quad \dist(a_n, q) \leq M.
\end{split}
\]
\end{definition}

In normed linear spaces, we can use a simpler criterion to check whether a sequence is bounded.
That is the content of the following proposition.

\begin{proposition}
Let $(V, \| \cdot \|)$ be a normed vector space.
Let $a : \mathbb{N} \to V$ be a sequence.
The sequence $a$ is bounded if and only if
\[
\begin{split}
& \existstext M > 0,\\
& \quad \foralltext n \in \mathbb{N},\\
& \quad\quad \left\| a_n \right\| \leq M.
\end{split}
\]
\end{proposition}

The proof is not difficult, but it is an excellent opportunity to go through it slowly, carefully following the proof expectations formulated in Chapter \ref{ch:proofs}.

\begin{proof}
We first show the ``if" part of the statement.
So we assume that
\[
\begin{split}
& \existstext M_1 > 0, \\
& \quad \foralltext n \in \mathbb{N}, \\
& \quad\quad \left\| a_n \right\| \leq M_1	
\end{split}
\]
and we need to show that
\[
\begin{split}
	& \existstext q \in V, \\
	& \quad \existstext M > 0, \\
	& \quad\quad \foralltext n \in \mathbb{N}, \\
	& \quad\quad\quad \left\| a_n - q \right\| \leq M.	
\end{split}
\]
Because the statement we need to show is of the form ``there exists a $q \in V$ such that \textellipsis", we need to choose an appropriate $q \in V$. 

We choose $q := 0$.

Next, we need to show ``there exists an $M > 0$ such that \textellipsis", so we need to identify an appropriate $M$.

For this, we first conclude by our assumption that there exists an $M_1 > 0$ such that for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, it holds that $\|a_n \| \leq M_1$. 

We choose $M := M_1$.

We need to show ``for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, \textellipsis". Therefore the next line of the proof is:

Let $n \in \mathbb{N}$. 

Finally we need to show that $\|a_n - q\| \leq M$.

But $\|a_n - q \| = \|a_n - 0 \| = \|a_n\|$ and we already know that $\| a_n \| \leq M_1$ and we chose $M := M_1$ so that indeed $\| a_n \| \leq M$.

Now we will show the ``only if" part of the statement.
We assume that 
\[
\begin{split}
	& \existstext q \in V, \\
	& \quad \existstext M > 0, \\
	& \quad\quad \foralltext n \in \mathbb{N}, \\
	& \quad\quad\quad \left\| a_n - q \right\| \leq M	
\end{split}
\]
and we need to show that
\[
\begin{split}
	& \existstext M_1 > 0, \\
	& \quad \foralltext n \in \mathbb{N}, \\
	& \quad\quad \left\| a_n \right\| \leq M_1.
\end{split}
\]
Can you see the template we should be using?

Choose $M_1 := M + \| q \|$.

Let $n \in \mathbb{N}$.

Then by the triangle inequality
\[
\begin{split}
\| a_n \| &= \|a_n - q + q \| \\
&\leq \| a_n - q \| + \|q\|\\
&\leq M + \|q\| = M_1.
\end{split}
\]
\end{proof}
