\begin{proposition}[uniqueness of limits]
	Let $(X, \dist)$ be a metric space and let $a : \mathbb{N} \to X$ be a sequence in $X$.
	Assume that $p, q \in X$ and assume that 
	\[
	\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n = p \quad \text{ and } \quad \lim_{n \to \infty} a_n = q.
	\]
	Then $p = q$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We argue by contradiction.
Suppose $p \neq q$.

Set $\epsilon := \dist(p,q)/2 > 0$.
Since $(a_n)$ converges to $p$, 
\begin{optional_text}
we know by the definition of convergence that
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:uniqueness-limits-aux}
\begin{split}
& \foralltext \epsilon_1 > 0,\\
&\quad \existstext N_1 \in \mathbb{N},\\
&\quad\quad \foralltext n \geq N_1,\\
&\quad\quad\quad \dist(a_n, p) < \epsilon_1.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Choose $\epsilon_1 := \epsilon$. Then we know that 
\end{optional_text}

there exists an $N_1 \in \mathbb{N}$ such that for every $n \geq N_1$, 
\[
\dist(a_n, p) < \epsilon.
\]
Since $(a_n)$ converges to $q$, there exists an $N_2 \in \mathbb{N}$ such that for every $n \geq N_2$,
\[
\dist(a_n, q) < \epsilon.
\]
Choose $N := \max(N_1, N_2)$. 
Then 
\[
\dist(p, q) \leq \dist(p, a_N) + \dist(a_N, q) < 2 \epsilon = \dist(p, q)
\]
which is a contradiction.
\end{proof}

Again, the lighter inset around statement (\ref{eq:uniqueness-limits-aux}) above denotes optional text. As we progress in these notes, we will more and more often omit it, but in the early stages it shows the argumentation a bit more clearly.
