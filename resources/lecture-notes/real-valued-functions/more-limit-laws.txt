\label{se:limit-laws-real-valued-functions}

If we need to show that a limit exists, or if we need to compute its value, we usually try to avoid going back to the formal definition of a limit. Instead, just as we did when we were working with sequences, we prefer to rely on limit laws.

The following theorem presents some limit laws for real-valued functions.

\begin{theorem}[Limit laws for real-valued functions]
	\label{th:limit-laws-real-valued-func}
Let $(X, \dist)$ be a metric space, let $D$ be a subset of $X$ and assume that $a \in D'$.
Let $f : D \to \mathbb{R}$ and $g : D \to \mathbb{R}$ be two real-valued functions and assume that $\lim_{x \to a} f(x)$ exists and equals $M \in \mathbb{R}$ and $\lim_{x \to a} g(x)$ exists and equals $L \in \mathbb{R}$. 
Then
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every $m \in \mathbb{N}$, the limit $\lim_{x \to a} (f(x))^m$ exists and equals $M^m$.
\item \label{it:limit-law-product-func} The limit $\lim_{x \to a} (f(x)g(x))$ exists and equals $M L$.
\item If $L \neq 0$, the limit
\[
\lim_{x \to a} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)}
\]
exists and equals $M / L$.
\item If for all $x \in D$, $f(x) \geq 0$, then for every $k \in \mathbb{N} \setminus \{0\}$,
\[
\lim_{x \to a} \sqrt[k]{f(x)} = \sqrt[k]{M}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The proof of this theorem follows from the sequence characterization of limits (Theorem \ref{th:seq-char-limits-func}), and from limit laws for sequences (Theorem \ref{th:limit-laws-real-valued-seq}). The proof of part (\ref{it:limit-law-product-func}) will be the aim of Exercise \ref{ex:limit-law-product-func}.
