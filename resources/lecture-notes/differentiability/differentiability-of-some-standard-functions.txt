We now record the fact that some functions, that you may know from Calculus, are differentiable on $\mathbb{R}$. Just like when we introduced Proposition \ref{pr:continuity-standard-function}, we are not even ready to define these functions, but I think it's useful to mention the result here anyways.

\begin{proposition}
For every $p \in \mathbb{R}$, the function $x \mapsto x^p$ is differentiable, with
\[
\frac{d}{dx} x^p = p \ x^{p-1}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}
	\label{pr:diff-standard-functions}
	The functions 
	\begin{align*}
		\mathrm{exp} &: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R} & \ln &: (0,\infty) \to \mathbb{R}\\
		\sin &: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R} & \cos &: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}\\
		\tan &: (-\pi/2, \pi/2) \to \mathbb{R} & \arctan &: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}	
	\end{align*}
	are all differentiable on their domain, while the functions
	\begin{align*}
		\arcsin &: [-1, 1] \to \mathbb{R}	& \arccos &: [-1, 1] \to \mathbb{R}
	\end{align*}
	are both differentiable on the interval $(-1, 1)$.
	
	The derivatives are given by:
	\begin{align*}
		\mathrm{exp}'(t) &= \exp(t) & \ln'(t) & = 1/ t\\
		\sin'(t) &=\cos(t) & \cos'(t) &= -\sin(t)\\
		\tan'(t) &= \frac{1}{\cos^2(t)} & \arctan'(t) & = \frac{1}{1 + t^2}\\
		\arcsin'(t) &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - t^2}} & \arccos'(t) &= - \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - t^2}}
	\end{align*}
\end{proposition}

We could also prove the chain rule, product, sum and quotient rules at this stage, but will do so only later in a somewhat larger generality. Nonetheless, in exercises you are free to use these theorems already.
