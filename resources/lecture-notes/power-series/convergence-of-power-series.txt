\begin{lemma}
Suppose a power series
\[
\sum_{k=0}^\infty a_k (x-c)^k
\]	
converges at a point $z \in \mathbb{R}$. Let $r > 0$ be such that $r < |z - c|$. Then the power series converges absolutely and uniformly on the interval $[c - r, c + r]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We will apply the Weierstrass $M$-test. 

Since the series
\[
\sum_{k=0}^\infty a_k (z - c)^k
\]
converges, the sequence $k \mapsto a_k (z-c)^k$ converges (to zero) and therefore it is bounded. In other words, there exists a $C > 0$ such that for all $k \in \mathbb{N}$,
\[
|a_k (z - c)^k| \leq C.
\]

Now note that for all $k \in \mathbb{N}$, and all $x \in [c - r, c + r]$,
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:ordered-behavior-convergence-series}
|a_k (x-c)^k| = |a_k| \left|\frac{x-c}{z-c} \right|^k |z - c|^k \leq C \left(\frac{r}{|z - c|}\right)^k =: M_k.
\end{equation}

Now note that since $r < |z - c|$, it follows that $r/|z -c| < 1$ so that
\[
\sum_{k=0}^\infty \left(\frac{r}{|z - c|}\right)^k
\]
is a standard convergent geometric series. By limit laws for series, the series
\[
\sum_{k=0}^\infty M_k = \sum_{k=0}^\infty C \left(\frac{r}{|z - c|}\right)^k
\]
is convergent as well.

By the Weierstrass $M$-test, the function series
\[
\sum_{k=0}^\infty a_k (x-c)^k
\]
converges absolutely and uniformly on the interval $[c - r, c + r]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
For every power series 
\[
\sum_{k=0}^\infty a_k (x - c)^k
\]
around a point $c \in \mathbb{R}$ exactly one of the following occurs:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The series converges for $x = c$ and diverges for $x \neq c$. In this case we say that the radius of convergence of the power series is $0$.
\item There exists an $R > 0$ such that for all $x \in (c - R, c + R)$ the power series converges and for all $x \in \mathbb{R} \backslash [c - R, c + R]$ the series diverges. In this case we say the radius of convergence equals $R$.
\item The series converges for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$. In this case we say the radius of convergence is $\infty$.	
\end{enumerate}	
\end{corollary}

The following proposition gives a way to determine the radius of convergence of a power series.

\begin{proposition}
	\label{pr:radius-of-convergence-limsup}
Let
\[
\sum_{k=0}^\infty a_k (x - c)^k
\]	
be a power series around $c$ and define the (extended real) number
\[
L := \limsup_{k \to \infty} \sqrt[k]{|a_k|}
\]
Then
\begin{enumerate}
\item if $L = \infty$, then the radius of convergence of the power series is $0$,
\item if $L \in (0,\infty)$, then the radius of convergence of the power series is $1/L$,
\item if $L = 0$, then the radius of convergence of the power series is $\infty$.	
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

For the proof of the proposition, let us first give an alternative version of the root test, Theorem \ref{th:root-test}.

\begin{theorem}[{Root test, $\limsup$ version}]
	Let $(b_k)$ be a sequence of nonnegative real numbers.
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item If 
		\[
		\limsup_{k \to \infty} \sqrt[k]{b_k} < 1,
		\]
		then the series $\sum_k b_k$ converges.
		\item If
		\[
		\limsup_{k \to \infty} \sqrt[k]{b_k} > 1
		\]
		then the series $\sum_k b_k$ diverges.
	\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
	First suppose that 
	\[
	\limsup_{k \to \infty} \sqrt[k]{b_k} < 1.
	\]
	Denote by $M := \limsup_{k \to \infty} \sqrt[k]{b_k}$. Then by the alternative characterization of the $\limsup$, there exists an $N \in \mathbb{N}$ such that for all $k \geq N$,
	\[
	\sqrt[k]{b_k} < M + \frac{1 - M}{2} = \frac{1 + M}{2} < 1.
	\]
	It follows by Theorem \ref{th:root-test} that the series $\sum_k b_k$ converges.
	
	Now suppose that
	\[
	\limsup_{k \to \infty} \sqrt[k]{b_k} = \infty.
	\]
	Then the sequence $(\sqrt[k]{b_k})$ is not bounded from above, and as a consequence the sequence $(b_k)$ is not bounded from above. Therefore, the series $\sum_k b_k$ diverges.
	
	If 
	\[
	M := \limsup_{k \to \infty} \sqrt[k]{b_k} \in (1, \infty)
	\]
	then by the alternative characterization of the $\limsup$, for every $K \in \mathbb{N}$, there exists an $\ell \geq K$ such that 
	\[
	\sqrt[\ell]{b_\ell} > 1.
	\]
	For such $\ell$, also $b_\ell > 1$. It follows that the sequence $(b_k)$ does not converge to zero, and therefore the series $\sum_k b_k$ diverges.
\end{proof}

With this version of the root test, we can now prove Proposition \ref{pr:radius-of-convergence-limsup}.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition \ref{pr:radius-of-convergence-limsup}]
We would like to apply the root test. We therefore consider
\[
\limsup_{k \to \infty} \sqrt[k]{|a_k (x - c)^k|} = \limsup_{k \to \infty} \left(\sqrt[k]{|a_k|} |x - c|\right)
\]	
It is clear that the power series always converges for $x = c$.

If $L = \infty$, and $x \neq c$, then 
\[
\limsup_{k \to \infty} \left( \sqrt[k]{|a_k|} |x - c| \right) = \infty
\] and in particular the terms do not converge to zero. Therefore, if $L = \infty$, the power series only converges for $x = c$.

If $L \in (0,\infty)$, the root test implies that the series converges if $|x - c| < 1/L$, then
\[
\limsup_{k \to \infty} \left( \sqrt[k]{|a_k|} |x - c| \right) < 1
\]
so that by the root test it follows that the series converges. On the other hand,if $L \in (0, \infty)$ and if $|x - c| > 1/ L$, then 
\[
\limsup_{k \to \infty} \left(\sqrt[k]{|a_k|} |x - c|\right) > 1
\]
and in particular the terms do not converge to zero. Therefore, the power series diverges.

If $L = 0$, it follows from the root test that the series converges for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$.
\end{proof}

Finally, although Proposition \ref{pr:radius-of-convergence-limsup} in theory always works for determining the radius of convergence of a power series, it can be useful to instead directly use the ratio test for determining for which $x$ the power series converges absolutely, and determining from there the radius of convergence. This method sometimes fails, but when it works it can be quite a bit easier.
