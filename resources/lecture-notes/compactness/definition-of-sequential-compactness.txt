In this short section, we define what it means for a subset of a metric space to be (sequentially) compact. We usually leave out the word `sequentially'.

\begin{definition}[(sequential) compactness]
Let $(X, \dist)$ be a metric space.
We say a subset $K \subset X$ is \emph{(sequentially) compact} if every sequence $x : \mathbb{N} \to K$ in $K$ has a 
converging subsequence $x \circ n$, converging to a point $z \in K$.
\end{definition}

The rest of the chapter will be devoted to deriving alternative characterizations of compactness. Especially in $(\mathbb{R}^d, \| \cdot \|)$, these alternative characterizations are a bit easier to deal with.
