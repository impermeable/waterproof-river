\begin{proposition}[Constant maps are differentiable]
	Let $(V, \| \cdot \|_V)$ and $(W, \|\cdot\|_W)$ be two normed vector spaces. Let $b \in W$ and consider the constant function $f : V \to W$ given by $f(v) = b$ for all $v \in V$. Then $f$ is differentiable and for all $a \in V$, $(Df)_a = 0$, i.e. it is the (linear) function that maps every element to $0$.
\end{proposition}

We now give a first example of a differentiable functions: linear functions are always differentiable.

\begin{proposition}[Linear maps are differentiable]
	Let $A : V \to W$ be a linear map between the finite-dimensional normed vector spaces $(V, \| \cdot \|_V)$ and $(W, \| \cdot \|_W)$. 
	Then the function $A : V \to W$ is differentiable on $V$ and for every $a \in V$ the derivative $(DA)_a \in \mathsf{Lin}(V, W)$ is just equal to $A$. Hence, the derivative of $A$ is the constant function $DA : V \to \mathsf{Lin}(V, W)$ given by
	\[
	a \mapsto A.
	\]
\end{proposition}
