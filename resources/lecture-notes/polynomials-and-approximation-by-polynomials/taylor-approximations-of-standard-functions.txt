When we apply the theorems of the previous sections to the standard functions, we get the following approximations.

\begin{corollary}
	\label{co:standard-taylor-polynomials}
For every $n \in \mathbb{N}$, it holds that
\[
\begin{split}
\exp(x) & = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \frac{x^k}{k!} + O(|x|^{n+1})	\\
\sin(x) &= \sum_{k=0}^{n} (-1)^k\frac{x^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!} + O(|x|^{2n+3})\\
\cos(x) &= \sum_{k=0}^n (-1)^k \frac{x^{2k}}{(2k)!} + O(|x|^{2n+2})\\
\ln(1 + x) &= \sum_{k=1}^n (-1)^{k+1} \frac{1}{k}x^k + O(|x|^{n+1})\\
\end{split}	
\]
\end{corollary}

The notation
\[
f(x) = g(x) + O(|x|^N)
\]
should be read as that there exists a $C \geq 0$ and a $\delta > 0$ such that for all $x \in (-\delta, \delta)$,
\[
| f(x) - g(x) | \leq C |x|^N.
\]
